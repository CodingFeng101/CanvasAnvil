# Slides

First get clear on what to say, then make every page beautiful.

## Three ways to begin

Your first sentence decides where it starts:

- **Idea**: just a topic, such as "brief new staff on our data security rules, about ten slides". The AI writes the outline for you.
- **Outline**: you already have an outline or a document. Paste it in and the AI arranges the pages from it.
- **Beautify**: you already have a finished deck and only want it to look better.

## Reviewing the outline

Before any picture is made, the outline is laid out for you: each slide's title and points. Add or remove slides, reorder them, rewrite them, and carry on when you are happy. Deleting a slide asks first.

> [!WHY]
> The costly part of a deck is what it says, not how it looks. Settle the structure in the cheap stage of words and you will not keep reworking pages in the expensive stage of images.

## Choosing a template

The template sets the style and palette of the whole deck; each slide's layout is designed for that slide's own content. The library holds more than two hundred templates, and you can upload your own. The library is stored apart from decks, so clearing or switching a deck leaves your templates in place.

## Generating

All slides generate at once, up to five at a time by default (changeable in Settings), and a slide that fails is retried automatically. Afterwards any single slide can be revised without touching the others.

## Export

- **PDF**
- **Image PPTX**: one picture per slide, exactly as previewed.
- **Editable PPTX**: each slide rebuilt from real text boxes, shapes and images, so you can keep changing the words in PowerPoint.

How decks are made is covered in [How decks are made](/docs/ppt-pipeline).
